2. Slider yaw under the measuring force -> Y-gib preload
---------------------------------------------------------
The workpiece pushes the moving jaw with F = 5.0 N (+x) at y -37.  The equal and opposite drive
force acts ABOVE the reference edge, so the two form a couple (the drive arm is not zero):
drive applied at                                 drive y  M N.mm  P min N
thumb roller (friction drive at the beam edge)      20.0     285     2.44
thumb on the roller hub / axle                      23.9     304     2.60
thumb on the right end face, mid-height             35.0     360     3.08
(plunger levers about the strip's far end, u 77.4: sum 116.8 mm; the strip lifts at its LEFT end.)
Design: 4.0 N each at u 14, 24 -> margin 1.64x for the roller drive, 1.30x for an end-face push.
Review fix (D4 + sliding force): both plungers moved LEFT of the battery pocket (were u 16 / 58.5, sum
  lever 81.5 mm, 6 N each).  The lever grows to 116.8 mm, so 4.0 N each keeps about the same
  margin as 6 N did (1.72x then, 1.64x now) for a third less load.
The other sense (strip lifting at its RIGHT end, pivot at the left end) has the short lever:
  sum (u - 0.5) = 37.0 mm.  No measuring load makes that moment (outside and inside
  measurements both lift the LEFT end), but a -y push on the slider's right end lifts the strip there
  above F = 2 P (mean lever) / 76.9 = 1.92 N.  Trade-off accepted: a grip that
  squeezes the slider's right end toward the jaws side (-y) with more than that tilts it while it lasts.
While seated, the yaw stiffness is the strip on the rail (brass on 1.8 mm printed rail): elastic-foundation estimate < 0.1 um at the jaw tip -- negligible next to section 1.

3. Slider roll under a press on the housing -> back-gib preload, back web, sliding force
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Back plungers at y 5 (review fix: were y 10), lever 13.8 mm to the upper pad line y 18.8.
ZERO/HOLD/MODE press (tact 1.6 N + finger): 2.5 N at y 31 -> 30.5 N.mm about the upper pad line; the back plungers hold the lower pads down if P >= 1.11 N each
grip on the top edge: 5.0 N at y 34 -> 78.0 N.mm about the upper pad line; the back plungers hold the lower pads down if P >= 2.83 N each
Design: back plungers 2.5 N each (was 4 N at y 10): margin 2.26x for a button press.  A press beyond 4.4 N at the top edge (y 34.4)
lifts the lower pads -- the reading is then wrong only while pressed (the gaps open, they do not close).

Sliding force: every preload acts on two faces (the plunger side and the reaction side), brass on ground
steel.  F = 2 x (2 P_back + 2 P_Y) x mu:
preloads                           N total  mu 0.12  mu 0.15  mu 0.20
first model: back 4 N, Y 6 N          20.0      4.8      6.0      8.0
now: back 2.5 N, Y 4.0 N              13.0      3.1      3.9      5.2
Target 2-5 N (commercial calipers).  mu 0.12-0.15 is brass on ground steel with a light oil film, 0.20 the
conservative value the first model used; dry brass on steel is 0.3-0.35 (keep the beam oiled).
  -> 3.9 N at mu 0.15 (5.2 N at 0.20), against 8.0 N before.
  Trade-off: the lower back preload accepts a pad lift above 4.4 N at the top edge (3.3 N at y 40
  in the 52 mm housing; the lower top edge has a shorter lever), and the Y preload's margin stays above 1.3x; the
  sliding force can be trimmed on the bench with the plunger set screws (0.4 mm per turn on the back plungers,
  0.5 mm on the Y plungers' nylon M3).
  O-ring roller rolling resistance is not included (TVP: measure; NBR at 0.15 squeeze is well under 0.5 N).

Back web (review fix a).  The back plungers now sit in the BODY's 3.2 back web, which closes the beam
tunnel behind the beam and joins the lower rail to the upper body (the first model put them in a back cover
screwed only at y 44: a 34 mm cantilever that bent 0.6-1.1 mm in PA-CF and lost the preload).
  PETG   web 3.2 thick, span 22.2 (rail to upper body), load 2.5 N at 6.0 from the rail, 20 mm strip:   0.89 um under the plunger
  PA-CF  web 3.2 thick, span 22.2 (rail to upper body), load 2.5 N at 6.0 from the rail, 20 mm strip:   0.36 um under the plunger
  FEA (fea_slider.py, whole body from step/slider_body.step, PA-CF, both preloads): plunger seat moves 1.85 um relative to the pads; reaction check 9.43 N
  -> the plunger travel (about 1 mm) is no longer used up by the housing: the preload is held as set.

4. Thermal error (zeroed at T0, read at T0 + dT)
-------------------------------------------------
The board is clamped at both end tabs (u 4.5 and 73.5); the AS5311 sits at u 39, exactly midway.  FR4 (E 20 GPa, 34 mm2) is far stiffer
than the printed towers in shear, so two bounds bracket the AS5311's position relative to the jaw face:
  A: the board follows the left clamp:  4.5 mm of body + 34.5 mm of FR4
  B: the board stays centred between the clamps: 39 mm of body (the midpoint moves with the body)
Fixed jaw: 5 mm of body from the dowels (x -5) to the face.  The tape is bonded to the beam (follows the steel).
body     beam      A um/K  B um/K fixed um/K  scale um/K @150 worst 10 K um
PETG     O1         0.429   2.223      0.095           -0.075          23.9
PETG     1.4301     0.234   2.028      0.070            0.675          27.7
PA-CF    O1         0.258   0.741      0.095           -0.075           9.1
PA-CF    1.4301     0.063   0.546      0.070            0.675          12.9
6061-T6  O1         0.229   0.491      0.063           -0.075           6.3
6061-T6  1.4301     0.034   0.296      0.038            0.675          10.1
Hand heat warms the slider 5-10 K within minutes (TVP); re-zero after handling.  The scale term is the classic
one: a 1.4301 beam reads a steel part short by 0.7 um/K at 150 mm -- O1 matches steel workpieces.
Bound B is the realistic one for a stiff board in soft towers; the first model's chain (body to a boss at
u 11, then FR4) no longer applies.  The difference between A and B is a reason to zero after warm-up.

    fitting (gives A to +/-0.005), depth-gauge the tape face below the beam face after bonding (+/-0.005),
    and lay 0-0.35 mm of Kapton shim (0.025 steps) under the tape so the predicted gap is 0.30.
    The channel (2.00 deep) gives 0.30 with NO shim at A max 1.20; a shim raises the tape,
    so every shorter package can be corrected (A nominal 1.10 -> 0.10 shim, A min 0.85 -> 0.35).

    tape face measured                                   +/-0.005  
    shim step / 2                                        +/-0.013  0.025 steps
    tape thickness along the length                      +/-0.020  TVP
    channel depth along the length                       +/-0.010

7. Envelope
------------
Housing envelope (u, y, z): 76.9 x 39.2 x 16.0 mm (SPEC 7: <= 78 x 39.5 x 16 since the 2026-11-09 slim-down, was <= 78 x 52 x 16; roller included).
Thumb roller rim at u 78.0 from the jaw face, 0.6 proud of the end face (review fix e: the end face moved 78.0 -> 77.4; the rim was at 78.6).

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

the board.  fea_slider.py solves the board (FR4, E 20 GPa, outline + tabs from board_parts.json) clamped at
